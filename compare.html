Shape complexity--

Model. Every planner defines an arrival time over the field; the formation at time t is the front where arrival = t. Drones are spaced evenly along the front, so each drone's swath (altitude) is its share of front length. Several front pieces = the swarm has split.

Peak swath: largest per-drone footprint needed (sets max altitude). Mission: path length of the fastest-moving drone. Speed spread: fastest/slowest drone on the front (1 = everyone flies at the same speed). Chord folds: fraction of MAT steps where consecutive perpendicular chords cross, meaning the rigid formation would sweep backwards. Gold = best in row. Drone areas: each colour is the region one drone is responsible for (its lane between neighbours on the front); the line of the same colour is that drone's flight path. Area balance: smallest / largest drone area. Transit jumps: times a drone must hop to a different piece of a split front.

Nadir camera. Arrows show each drone's heading (direction of travel); the white rectangle is its downward camera footprint, 2·h·tan(HFOV/2) across track by 2·h·tan(VFOV/2) along track, rotated to the heading. Altitude h is the lowest that lets the footprint reach every cell of that drone's area, plus the overlap margin. The footprints are then stamped along every path; any field cell still unimaged raises the altitude of the drone that owns it, and this repeats until Nadir coverage = 100%. "Final image overlap" shows unimaged cells in red (there should be none) and how many drones image each cell. Compute ms: server time for that planner only.

Complexity score (0 = disk, 100 = very complex) weights non-convexity 30%, boundary roughness 25%, elongation 15%, width variation along the medial trunk 15%, and necks (pinch points in inscribed radius) 15%.
